- [ ] **Policy head, gated on the multimodality diagnostic above (decided
      2026-08-27):**
  - Tight clustering (no real multimodality) → a **single Beta distribution
    per dimension** (not plain point-regression). Native `[0, 1]` support
    matches `x`'s domain exactly (no Gaussian+squash artifacts), and it's
    the distributional head M5.5 needs anyway for a differentiable
    `log π(x)` — strictly better than point-regression even without
    multimodality.
  - Genuinely separated clusters → a **mixture of per-dimension Beta
    products** (mixture-of-Beta instead of the usual mixture-of-diagonal-
    Gaussian MDN) — same bounded-support benefit, plus multimodality via the
    mixture. Standard MDN practice already accepts the per-component
    diagonal/factorized approximation (correlation gets captured via the
    mixture itself, not within a single component) — same tradeoff a
    factorized bar distribution would have paid, without that option's
    combinatorial binning cost.
  - **Either variant needs the `other_diff` prototype's documented Beta
    reparameterization fix**: `archive/src/prototype/other_diff/README.md`
    found real NaN-gradient instability when α or β drop below 1.0 during
    training (the Beta becomes U-shaped, spikes at the boundaries) — clamp
    via `softplus(x) + 1.0`, not a bare `softplus`.
  - Rejected regardless of diagnostic outcome: factorized bar distribution
    over `x`'s dims (combinatorially worse than Beta for the same
    independence-assumption cost), full flow matching (disproportionate
    machinery for a single-oracle deterministic target, not genuine
    multi-demonstrator multimodality).
  - Make sure whichever variant is built supports evaluating the policy
    density π(x | context) at arbitrary query points on a grid, not just
    sampling — needed for M5's interpretability diagnostic
